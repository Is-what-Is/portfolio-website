### Supply Chain Optimisation App

This app simulates one manufacturer under normal conditions. It finds the safety stock, capacity and feedback setting that give the lowest total cost. The fill rate must meet a target.

The app shows four effects:

- The trade-off between inventory variance and order variance.
- Safety stock as a buffer against demand and lead-time uncertainty.
- Capacity as a buffer against order variability.
- The effect of the order cadence on production stability.

### Dynamics

Demand and lead times are random. Each day, the simulation does these steps in this sequence:

1. The manufacturer receives orders that arrive on that day.
2. Demand occurs.
3. The manufacturer supplies today's demand first, then any backlog.
4. On a review day, the manufacturer places a new order.

Orders can arrive out of sequence. The production of each order is spread evenly over one review period.

### Order policy

The manufacturer uses the Proportional Order Up-To (POUT) policy:

$$
\text{Order} = c\,\mu_d + \frac{ss - NS}{T} + \frac{\mu_L\,\mu_d - WIP}{T}
$$

- $c\,\mu_d$ is the mean demand for one review period.
- $NS$ is net stock (inventory minus backlog).
- $WIP$ is the sum of orders placed but not received.
- $T$ is the proportional feedback controller.

$T$ controls how fast the manufacturer corrects errors:

- A small $T$ keeps inventory stable but makes orders unstable.
- A large $T$ keeps orders stable but makes inventory unstable.
- $T = 1$ gives the standard Order Up-To policy.
- $T \le 0.5$ makes the system unstable.

Theory gives $\text{Bullwhip} = 1 / (2T - 1)$. The simulation agrees with this result.

### Optimal safety stock

The cost-only optimum is the $b / (b + h)$ quantile of the net stock shortfall. If this value does not meet the fill-rate target, the app increases the safety stock until the target is met.

The app also shows King's formula as a reference:

$$
ss = z \sqrt{(L + R)\,\sigma_d^2 + (\sigma_L\,\mu_d)^2}, \qquad z = \Phi^{-1}(\text{target})
$$

King's formula uses the cycle service level, which is the probability of no stockout in a cycle. The app uses the fill rate, which is the fraction of demand supplied from stock. Thus, King's formula usually gives a higher value.

With a monthly cadence, the optimal safety stock can be negative. Cycle stock supplies most of the demand, so a small planned shortage at the end of each cycle costs less than extra stock.

### Optimal capacity

One more unit of capacity costs $u$ each day. It saves $w$ on each day with overtime. Thus, the optimal capacity is the $1 - u/w$ quantile of daily production:

$$
k = \mu_d + z\,\sigma_P, \qquad z = \Phi^{-1}(1 - u/w)
$$

- If $w < 2u$, the optimal capacity is less than the mean production.
- If $w > 2u$, the optimal capacity is more than the mean production.

### Cadence

The cadence sets how often the manufacturer orders: daily ($c = 1$), weekly ($c = 7$) or monthly ($c = 30$). A longer cadence makes daily production more stable, so the manufacturer needs less spare capacity. But a longer cadence also increases cycle stock, which increases holding cost.

### Optimisation method

The optimiser finds the safety stock ($ss$), capacity ($k$) and $T$ with the lowest expected cost over 365 days:

1. For each $T$ from 0.1 to 10, it simulates 30 replications.
2. For each $T$, it calculates the optimal capacity and safety stock directly from the results.
3. It selects the best $T$ in the range 1 to 10.
4. It refines that $T$ with a bounded scalar search.

Orders do not depend on safety stock. Thus, one simulation for each $T$ is sufficient. All parameter sets use the same random numbers, so the comparison is fair. One optimisation takes approximately one second.

### How to use the app

1. Set the inputs.
2. Select the cadence.
3. Push **Optimise**. The app puts the optimal values into the parameter boxes and shows the cost and fill rate.
4. Examine the charts of Bullwhip, inventory variance and cost against $T$.
5. To test different values, push **Edit** and change the parameters.
6. Push **Run** to simulate one year with the current parameters.
7. Compare the cost with the optimum.
8. Download the results as a CSV file.
